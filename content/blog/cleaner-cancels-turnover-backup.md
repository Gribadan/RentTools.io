The cleaner cannot attend, checkout is approaching and another guest is due today. Adding a different name to the schedule is a useful start, but it does not establish who will arrive, whether they can enter or when the home will be ready.

Treat the change as a handover with a deadline. The immediate job is to secure a confirmed visit and a credible readiness check, while keeping the guest informed if the agreed arrival is at risk. This is an operational plan, not a promise that a spare cleaner will always be available.

## Establish the deadline and the remaining work

Confirm the outgoing guest's actual departure, the next reservation's agreed check-in and whether anyone has inspected the home. Do not assume a routine clean is enough before you know its condition. Include linen, restocking, travel, access and the final inspection in the plan. The [turnover-window guide](/blog/check-in-check-out-time-turnover-math) helps separate the available hours from the work that must fit inside them.

**Illustrative timing:** check-in is at 16:00. A replacement can start at 14:00, with an assumed 90 minutes of cleaning and 30 minutes for the final check. That reaches 16:00 with zero contingency. These are planning assumptions, not cleaning standards or measured averages. A delay collecting linen or getting inside would make the plan late; do not call it comfortably covered.

Airbnb's [ground rules for hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895) require a clean home before check-in. Finding cover does not replace that readiness requirement. Set a decision point early enough to act if the replacement cannot confirm a workable visit.

## Ask for a commitment, not just availability

Contact the backup person or service with the specific property, access window, expected work and deadline. Ask who is actually attending and when that person can start. An agency accepting a request is not the same as a worker confirming arrival.

Have one person coordinate the change. Confirm the replacement's other jobs and travel before moving a different property's visit. Otherwise one cancellation can quietly become two missed turnovers. If the route is tight, review the [two-properties, one-cleaner checklist](/blog/one-cleaner-two-rental-turnovers).

Keep searching or escalate to the person responsible for the booking if nobody can accept the real scope and timing. Do not mark a visit covered because a message was sent or a colleague said they would ask around.

## Hand over enough information to finish the visit

Send a short, property-specific brief through the team's agreed private channel:

- Which departure and next arrival the visit serves, and the deadline for reporting readiness.
- What cleaning, linen and restocking are required, including any known exception.
- How the authorised person can enter and where the required supplies are available.
- Who checks the result and who receives a problem report if the scope is larger than expected.

Ask the replacement to acknowledge that brief. A familiar cleaner may know the home; a substitute may not know where spare sheets are or which door to use. Resolve those questions before their planned start, rather than counting that time as cleaning.

RentTools supports cleaning records with a date, status and notes. Keep the relevant record pending while the visit is still unverified and use its notes for the operational handover. A record in the [cleaning schedule](/blog/cleaning-schedule-automation) helps the team share the plan; direct confirmation from the attending person is still necessary.

## Tell the guest when the plan is at risk

If the numbers do not fit, contact the guest before they reach the door. State what is known, what remains uncertain and when you will give the next update. Do not explain the cleaner's personal circumstances or offer an earlier arrival simply to make the message sound reassuring.

**Example wording, to adapt only if accurate:** “There is a risk the home will not be ready for your 16:00 arrival. I am arranging replacement cleaning and will update you by 14:30.” Choose an update time you can meet and follow through even if there is no final answer yet.

Use the booking platform's conversation for the relevant reservation. Airbnb's [cleanliness-issue guidance](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2993) tells guests to keep communication there so the exchange is recorded. If a workable arrival cannot be provided, use the platform's support process and discuss the actual options; do not ask the guest to cancel just to conceal a host-side problem.

## Close the loop with a readiness report

Get confirmation that the work is finished and the home has passed the agreed check. Record unresolved issues before deciding that arrival can proceed. Only then mark the cleaning done and send an accurate readiness update. If the guest arrives before that point, use the [early-arrival response plan](/blog/guest-arrives-before-cleaning).

Tell the original cleaner and any other contacted backup who is now responsible, so a second person does not turn up for the same job. Afterwards, note what slowed the handover and keep a realistic backup contact plan for the next turnover. The useful outcome is a checked home and a clear owner for the visit, not merely a full-looking schedule.
