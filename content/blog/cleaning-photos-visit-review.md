Illustrative situation: a cleaner sends a made-bed photo and a tidy bathroom view. The home has two bathrooms, and the host is managing several properties that afternoon. Before replying “ready”, the host needs to know which visit the pictures show and what happened in the bathroom that is not pictured.

A photo review works best when it answers a small set of agreed questions. This is an operational check for an ordinary turnover, separate from documenting a damage claim or preparing marketing images.

## Agree what the photos should help you check

Start with the actual cleaning assignment and the sleeping arrangement promised for the next stay. Identify useful views of the areas that need review, including any previously open item. Ask for enough context to recognize the room rather than a collection of close-ups with no location.

Airbnb’s [ground rules for home hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895) require cleaning between stays. Your photo review supports checking the work; the number of pictures is a team decision, not a substitute for the cleaning itself.

Arrange the visit and confirm departure before asking someone to enter for pictures. If occupancy is unclear, use the [checkout-confirmation handover](/blog/checkout-confirmation-cleaning-handover). An old room photo or a calendar checkout is not a current occupancy report.

## Tie the pictures to this property and visit

Ask the sender to identify the property, cleaning date and room in the team’s agreed handover. Confirm whether the images were taken during that visit. A message arriving today does not establish when the attached picture was taken, especially if it was forwarded from another conversation.

If something does not match the property or the assigned work, ask the person who took the pictures to clarify it. Request a fresh view of the unresolved area when appropriate. Do not rename an old photo to make it look current or edit unfinished work out of the image. Where an image cannot settle the question, arrange a direct check.

**Illustrative example:** one photo shows one of two bathrooms. It may help review that room, but leaves the other bathroom unconfirmed. The missing view is a follow-up question, not evidence that the second bathroom was skipped.

Use the image and the remaining question together:

| Photo shows | Still check |
|---|---|
| Made bed | Linen and other beds |
| Bathroom view | Other bathroom and supplies |
| Kitchen overview | Unseen areas and open work |

A wide shot does not show every surface, confirm that linen was changed or establish that an appliance works. Keep these limits in the review instead of treating an attractive image as a complete inspection. For a supply shortage, use the [restocking check](/blog/restock-check-before-guest-arrival) to confirm what is actually in place.

## Keep the frame useful and the sharing limited

Photograph the work area after the agreed entry arrangements are satisfied. Keep people, identity documents, messages, access codes and personal belongings out of routine room views. Choose another angle or describe the remaining issue when a photograph would expose unnecessary personal details.

Airbnb’s [physical-space privacy policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3060) requires guest permission for host entry during a reservation, except in an emergency. Taking a routine handover picture does not create that exception.

Use the team’s agreed storage and share only with the people who need the material for the handover. Keep the original image there if a follow-up depends on it. A general coordination note can say where the authorized team can find it without copying private guest information into the note or a public post.

## Record what was checked and what remains open

RentTools cleaning records have a date, status and notes. Put a short review outcome beside the relevant visit: which area was checked, what remains unresolved, who will check it and when. The note does not verify image age, perform a photo inspection or confirm that a colleague read it. Record the outcome here and keep the images in the team’s agreed storage.

A useful outcome distinguishes “pictures received”, “review completed” and “remaining work confirmed”. If a missing towel or unfinished bed is found, assign that action and confirm the result. If the review exposes a readiness problem, use the [maintenance-hold and reopening check](/blog/maintenance-calendar-hold-reopening) for the separate availability decision.

Close the follow-up when the person responsible has confirmed the open items and the next-arrival plan is clear. For your next review, [open RentTools](/onboard) and add one factual note to the correct cleaning date, including the next check if anything is still unknown.
