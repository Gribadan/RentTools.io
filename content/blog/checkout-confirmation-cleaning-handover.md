Checkout time has passed, the cleaner is waiting and no departure message has arrived. That silence does not tell you whether the guests have left, agreed a later time or simply forgotten to send a note. Sending someone straight inside turns an information gap into a difficult handover.

Start by establishing the actual agreement and the current situation. The aim is to give the attending cleaner a clear instruction and preserve a realistic plan for the next stay, without accusing a guest of overstaying on the strength of a missing message.

## Check the agreement before interpreting the silence

Open the reservation and the latest conversation. Confirm the correct property, departure date and agreed checkout time, including any exception accepted by a host or co-host. Compare those details with what the cleaner was told. A default time on a property card may not describe this particular stay.

Airbnb's [checkout guidance](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3501) puts checkout details in the reservation and recommends a departure message. It does not make the absence of that message proof that someone is still inside.

**Illustrative situation:** the listing normally has an 11:00 checkout, but a 12:00 departure was agreed for this reservation. A cleaner following the old plan arrives at 11:10. The first problem is the outdated handover, not evidence of a late guest. These times are an example, not a default policy or a real booking.

Where a one-off change is agreed, record it in the appropriate booking settings and team plan. Airbnb documents [changing an individual reservation's checkout time](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/4181). Do not change a property's general default merely to describe one exception, or assume the cleaner learned about a private guest conversation.

## Ask for the missing fact with a short message

Contact the guest through the reservation's conversation. Ask whether checkout is complete and what remains to be agreed if they are still there. Keep the message factual; avoid sending a demand for new cleaning chores because the team is under time pressure.

**Example wording to adapt:** “Could you please confirm whether everyone has checked out and the agreed key return is complete? I am coordinating the cleaning visit and want to give the team the correct update.” This is sample wording, not an actual guest exchange.

Give one host or co-host responsibility for following up. If there is no answer, check the existing checkout arrangement and use the booking platform's support process as needed. Do not turn silence into either an automatic late-checkout charge or an instruction for the cleaner to resolve an occupancy dispute at the door.

If the guest confirms they have left but has left belongings or a different access arrangement, clarify the remaining handover before describing the home as clear for a routine turnover. Keep that conversation private and give the cleaner only the information needed for the visit.

## Give the cleaner a clear start instruction

A calendar checkout and a working key do not by themselves establish the current occupancy or the agreed permission for a visit. Airbnb's [privacy policy for physical spaces](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3060) requires guest permission for host access during a reservation, except in an emergency. Do not treat routine cleaning as that exception.

For this ordinary turnover, make the host responsible for resolving uncertainty. Tell the cleaner whether the visit can start, must wait or has been rescheduled, and when the next update will arrive. If they are already outside, ask them to contact the coordinator rather than improvise entry or confront a guest.

The handover should settle four practical points:

- Which property and departure the visit concerns.
- What confirms that the agreed turnover can begin, including any access instructions still needed.
- Who is attending and who receives a report if the actual condition changes the job.
- When the work and final readiness check must be reviewed against the next arrival.

RentTools supports cleaning records with a date, status and notes. Keep the relevant record pending while the visit is waiting or unverified, and add the revised plan to its notes. Send the attending person the update and ask for acknowledgement; a note in the [cleaning schedule](/blog/cleaning-schedule-automation) is useful shared context, not proof that a message was received.

## Reassess readiness when the start changes

Once the handover is settled, compare the actual possible start with the remaining cleaning, linen, restocking and inspection work. The [turnover-window guide](/blog/check-in-check-out-time-turnover-math) helps separate the available hours from the work still required. A later start should trigger a fresh plan, not an unchanged promise to the arriving guest.

If the next arrival may be affected, communicate the known situation and a reliable update time before the guest reaches the door. Do not confirm an early arrival while readiness remains uncertain. If the guest arrives before preparation is finished, use the [early-arrival response plan](/blog/guest-arrives-before-cleaning).

Mark the cleaning done only after the work and agreed check are complete, rather than when departure is confirmed. Record who confirmed the handover and who reported readiness. Those two confirmations close different parts of the task and give the team a clear basis for the next arrival.
