The cleaner sends a photo from the kitchen: a crack across the glass hob, a burn mark on the worktop next to it, and a bin bag of broken glass by the door. Checkout was at 11:00. The next guests land at 16:00. Two clocks started the moment that photo arrived: the arrival in a few hours and the platform's window for asking the departed guest to pay.

Most hosts handle the money first and the arrival second. Reverse that order. The steps below record the damage so a claim stays possible, settle whether the next guest can still arrive, and then file within each platform's deadline without losing the evidence along the way.

## Record the damage before anything is moved

Give the person who finds the damage one instruction: photograph first, touch second. A usable record covers:

- The property, the date and the room, so the find is tied to the right departure.
- One wide photo that shows where the damage sits, then close-ups of the damage itself.
- A short factual description: what is damaged, whether it still works, and whether anything is missing.
- The broken item itself, kept until the claim is closed unless it is dangerous to store.
- Who recorded it and where the photos are stored.

Photograph objects, not people or personal belongings. A cracked hob is evidence; a guest's open suitcase is not. If a guest's item is mixed in with the damage, the [found-item routine](/blog/guest-left-items-behind) applies to that item separately.

If you keep turnover photos of the main rooms after each clean, the previous set shows the hob intact at the last departure. That before-and-after pair answers the question every platform asks: did this happen during this stay? Without it you are relying on memory and the cleaner's word.

RentTools stores each cleaning record with a date, a status and free-text notes, and the record can hold photos. Attaching the damage photos and a one-line description to that turnover keeps the evidence next to the right property and day. The record is shared context for your team; it does not file a claim or notify the guest.

## Decide whether the next guest can still arrive

Before you open any platform's claim form, settle the arrival. The question is not "how much will this cost" but "can the home be used as described tonight". Sort the damage into one of three levels:

| Damage level | Arrival |
|---|---|
| Cosmetic | Go ahead; tell the guest |
| Functional | Fix or substitute first |
| Safety | Do not host until fixed |

Cosmetic damage, such as a scratched table or a stained cushion, does not stop the stay. Mention it in the arrival message so the next guest does not report it as their own.

Functional damage removes something the listing promises. A cracked hob means no cooking; a broken washing machine means no laundry. Decide whether you can restore the function before check-in, offer a substitute such as a portable induction plate, or tell the guest in advance and agree an adjustment. Tell them before arrival, not after they discover it. The [refund guide](/blog/airbnb-guest-refund-compensation-math) covers how to size a goodwill adjustment when a promised amenity is missing.

Safety damage, such as exposed wiring, a broken lock, a gas smell or shattered glass still in the room, means the home is not ready. Block the nights the repair needs and treat the arriving guest as a separate problem: rebooking them is covered in the [relocation guide](/blog/overbooking-relocation-cost-math). A damaged home that is closed for repair is a [maintenance hold](/blog/maintenance-calendar-hold-reopening), and the same rules apply: close the dates on every channel and reopen only after a check.

**Illustrative example:** the cleaner reports the cracked hob at 11:40. You phone an appliance engineer who can replace the glass top on Thursday. The guests arrive today at 16:00 for three nights. You cannot restore cooking before check-in, so you deliver a two-ring induction plate before 15:00, message the guests about the temporary setup, and keep the engineer's quote for the claim. The times and the substitute are assumptions for the example, not a recommended standard.

## Know the clock on each platform

All three major platforms give you the same headline window: 14 days after the guest's checkout. The arrival is almost always sooner than that, which is why the evidence has to be gathered on turnover day even if you file later in the week.

| Platform | Deadline | First step |
|---|---|---|
| Airbnb | 14 days | Resolution Center |
| Booking.com | 14 days | Damage request |
| Vrbo | 14 days | Deposit claim |

**Airbnb.** Host damage protection, part of AirCover for Hosts, asks you to [file a reimbursement request in the Resolution Center within 14 days of the responsible guest's checkout](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/279). The guest has 24 hours to respond. If they decline, pay only part or do not answer, you can escalate to a Host damage protection request, which Airbnb Support reviews. Airbnb lists photos, videos, repair or cleaning estimates and receipts as supporting documentation, and excludes normal wear and tear and routine checkout cleaning such as laundry, dishes or trash. Airbnb states that this is not insurance.

**Booking.com.** Outside the United States, a partner chooses between a damage deposit they manage themselves and the damage programme that Booking.com facilitates. Under the programme you [submit a damage payment request within 14 days after the guest checks out](https://partner.booking.com/en-gb/help/policies-payments/guest-payments/everything-you-need-know-about-damage-policy-options); Booking.com then contacts the guest, and an accepted request is paid to you by bank transfer, up to the maximum you selected when you set the programme up. The guest-facing [damage policy notice](https://www.booking.com/content/damage-policy.html) adds that the guest can reject a claim and that general cleaning, ordinary wear and tear and non-physical damage such as smoking fines are not covered.

**Vrbo.** After checkout you have [14 days to assess the property and file a damage claim](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-file-a-damage-deposit-claim) against a refundable deposit or a card on file. The claim cannot exceed the deposit amount, and you cannot withhold more after the first claim, so file once, with the full amount you can document. If the guest bought a Property Damage Protection plan instead, the [claim goes through that plan](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-protect-my-property-from-potential-damage) and is paid under its terms and coverage limit.

Check the current help page for the platform where the stay was booked before you rely on any of these windows. The texts above were read on 9 November 2026; platform rules change, and local consumer law can add requirements of its own. For a comparison of what each scheme actually pays out, see the [damage protection comparison](/blog/airbnb-aircover-vs-booking-damage-deposit).

## Message the guest before you file

Every platform's process starts, formally or informally, with the guest hearing from you. Airbnb's flow begins with a request to the guest. Vrbo tells hosts to communicate in writing through its messaging and sends your claim description to the guest. Booking.com contacts the guest on your behalf under the damage programme, but a short factual message from you first still avoids a surprise.

Write it in the booking conversation, not by text message or a number you looked up. Keep it factual and attach the photos. **Example wording to adapt:** "Our cleaning team found the glass hob cracked and a burn mark on the worktop after your checkout this morning. I have attached photos. The repair quote is $240, and I will send the request through the platform today. Let me know if you have any information about how it happened." This is sample wording, not a real guest exchange.

Do not accuse, do not estimate before you have a quote, and do not include the cleaner's name or phone number. If the guest replies with an explanation that changes the picture, record it in the turnover note before you file.

## Separate the repair from the cleaning record

The cleaning record answers one question: is the home ready for the next guest? Close it as done or skipped with a note that points to the damage, and open the repair as its own task with a person, a date and a cost. Leaving the turnover marked pending because an engineer is coming on Thursday hides the fact that the clean itself was finished.

Keep the paperwork for the repair with the claim: the quote, the invoice and the receipt for any substitute you bought. Airbnb explicitly lists estimates and receipts among the supporting documents, and the other platforms ask for the amount you are requesting. A claim filed on day 13 with a dated invoice beats one filed on day 2 with a guess.

When the repair is finished, add the final cost and the outcome of the claim to the same turnover note: "Hob replaced on [date], request accepted, paid out on [date]" or "Request declined, cost absorbed". The next time something breaks in that room, the history is in one place.

A recurring break usually points at the setup, not the guest. If the same glass shelf cracks twice a year, replace it with something sturdier rather than filing a third claim.

Recording damage, deciding the arrival and filing on time all depend on the turnover being written down somewhere the whole team can see. ## FAQ

**How long do I have to report guest damage on Airbnb?**

Airbnb asks you to file a reimbursement request in the Resolution Center within 14 days of the responsible guest's checkout. The guest then has 24 hours to respond before you can escalate to a Host damage protection request. File earlier if the next guest arrives sooner, because evidence gathered after another stay is much harder to attribute.

**Does Host damage protection cover normal wear and tear?**

No. Airbnb's help page excludes normal wear and tear and routine checkout cleaning such as laundry, dishes or trash removal. A scuffed wall after a normal stay is a maintenance cost; a hole punched in it is damage. Photograph both, but only file for the second.

**Can I charge a Booking.com guest for damage after checkout?**

Not directly. If you use the damage programme, you submit a damage payment request within 14 days after checkout and Booking.com contacts the guest; an accepted request is paid to you by bank transfer up to the maximum you chose at setup. If you run your own damage deposit instead, you manage the process yourself under the terms you set.

**What if the repair costs more than the Vrbo deposit?**

A Vrbo damage deposit claim cannot exceed the deposit amount, and you cannot withhold more after the first claim. File once for the documented amount up to the deposit. For anything above it, you are relying on the guest's goodwill, a Property Damage Protection plan if the guest bought one, or your own insurance.

**Should the cleaner clean up the damage or leave it?**

Photograph first, then make the home safe. Broken glass on the floor must go before a guest walks in, but the photos should show it where it was found and the broken item should be kept until the claim is closed. Repairs that are not needed for safety or for tonight's arrival can wait until the evidence is recorded.

**What if I only notice the damage after the next guest has checked in?**

You can still file within the platform's window, but you now have two stays that could be responsible and no photo separating them. That is the strongest argument for a short photo routine at every turnover: the previous set proves the condition at the last departure.
