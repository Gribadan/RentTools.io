You spend a few nights in your own rental, leave it reasonably tidy and head home. The next guest booking is already on the calendar. What may be missing is the handover between those two uses: the beds you slept in, the towels in the laundry basket and the keys now in your travel bag.

An owner stay does not need a guest-facing booking to create preparation work. The useful question is not whether it generated a checkout event, but whether the home is ready for the next promised stay.

## Start with the actual end of personal use

Confirm when everyone using the home will leave, not just the last night that was blocked. Tell the person doing the reset when they can enter and whether belongings will remain for collection. If departure changes, update that instruction directly rather than assuming a calendar change was noticed.

Keep the personal-use period distinct from the cleaning appointment. A block protects availability; an appointment tells someone when work is expected. Neither one confirms that the other has happened.

For an existing guest reservation, preserve its agreed arrangements. If your departure would leave too little preparation time, resolve the conflict before telling the incoming guest that access is ready.

## Hand over what changed inside the home

Start with the areas actually used, then review the whole setup promised to the next guest. Mention beds slept in, towels used, food or dishes left behind, moved furniture and items taken out of normal storage. The person cleaning should not have to reconstruct your stay from what they find.

Airbnb's [ground rules for home hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895) require a clean listing before check-in and cleaning between stays. This guide's owner-stay handover is a practical way to prepare the home; it is not a separate platform rule about how to label personal use.

**Illustrative example:** a two-bedroom rental was used by the owner, who slept in one bedroom and stored bags in the other. The sleeping room needs the appropriate linen change, while the second room still needs its guest setup checked. Do not describe the second room as untouched simply because nobody slept there.

Ask for confirmation of the work, not just a message saying that someone visited. If bedding is still away at the laundry, use the [linen-delivery check](/blog/linen-delivery-delay-checkin) to decide how every promised bed will be made.

## Restore the guest setup, not just the appearance

Put guest supplies back where the arrival instructions say they are. Check the items you used during your own stay against your normal preparation list, then follow the [restocking check](/blog/restock-check-before-guest-arrival) for shortages. A shopping order is still different from supplies being delivered and put away.

Remove personal belongings from guest areas and return any items you moved. Verify the practical entry route too: where the guest key is, whether the agreed handover is possible and who will resolve a missing item. Do not place access codes or private personal details in a shared cleaning note.

If you discovered a fault while staying there, give it an owner and a next check. A tidy room does not establish that the fault has been fixed. For work that prevents hosting, use the separate [maintenance-hold and reopening check](/blog/maintenance-calendar-hold-reopening).

## Put the reset on the correct date

RentTools supports manually marked cleaning dates and cleaning records with a status and notes. Use the correct property's cleaning date to make the reset visible, and add the remaining work and next check. A manual entry is useful when you need an explicit task rather than relying on an imported reservation to explain the visit.

Keep the instruction factual: areas used, work needed, who will confirm completion and when. Contact the person responsible through your agreed channel. A saved note is not evidence that the cleaner has accepted the appointment or read the changes.

Mark work complete after it has been confirmed. If something remains unresolved, leave a clear next action instead of allowing a completed status to stand in for readiness.

## Check the next arrival and availability separately

Confirm the home is prepared for the next booking before promising access. Then check the relevant selling calendars if dates need to become available again. Airbnb's [calendar-sync guidance](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/99) explains that blocks made on another calendar may or may not block Airbnb. Check the destination calendar itself rather than inferring its state from one view.

For the next owner stay, [open RentTools](/onboard) and plan the departure-to-cleaning handover when arranging personal use. One explicit reset date and one confirmed result are more useful than hoping the gap between calendar entries explains the work.
