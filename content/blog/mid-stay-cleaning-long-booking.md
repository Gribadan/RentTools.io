Illustrative situation, not a customer case: a guest books 35 nights. The cleaning plan for the home shows nothing between arrival and departure, because every cleaning on it follows a checkout. On day 16 the guest asks whether anyone is coming to change the sheets. The same morning the cleaner asks whether she is still expected at that address this month.

A long stay does not need a turnover, but it usually needs a visit. That visit has to be offered before the booking, agreed with the guest each time and written down as a task of its own.

## Decide what the visit covers before you offer it

A mid-stay visit happens in a home someone is living in. The guest's clothes are in the wardrobe, their laptop is on the table and their food is in the fridge. That changes the job. Fix the scope in advance so the cleaner does not have to guess on the doorstep:

| Area | Mid-stay visit |
|---|---|
| Bed linen and towels | Swap for a clean set |
| Kitchen and bathroom | Clean surfaces and floors |
| Guest belongings | Do not move or open |
| Supplies | Restock what was promised |
| Visible faults | Note and report to the host |

The linen swap only works if a clean set exists besides the one on the bed. Count that into the [linen inventory](/blog/linen-inventory-short-term-rental) before you promise it.

There is no universal rule for how often to come. **Illustrative example:** for a 35-night stay, visits on day 12 and day 24 split the stay into three roughly equal parts. That spacing is an assumption for planning, not a standard. A 28-night stay may need one visit; a guest who cooks every day may ask for more. Whether long stays are worth taking at all is a pricing question covered in the [mid-term rental guide](/blog/mid-term-rental-math).

## Put the offer in the listing, not in a mid-stay message

A visit the guest learns about on day 10 feels like an inspection. A visit described before they booked is a service.

Airbnb's [guidance on monthly stays](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2729) says a host who offers a cleaning during the stay should add it to the listing description before a reservation is booked, including how often it is offered and on what day and time. If the cleaning is not part of the total price, the extra cost should be stated up front, and the guest chooses whether to accept it.

For a booking from another platform or a direct booking, read that channel's current rules and state the same three things in writing before the stay is confirmed:

- How often the visit happens.
- The day and time window.
- Whether it is included or costs extra, and how much.

Do not turn an undisclosed visit into a condition after the booking. If the listing said nothing, offer the visit as an option the guest can decline. Page checked on 10 November 2026.

## Ask for permission before every entry

An agreement in the listing does not open the door on the day. The same Airbnb guidance says the host needs to message the guest and get permission for the cleaner to enter before each cleaning, and that the cleaner should not be let in if the guest has not confirmed. Airbnb's [privacy rules](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3060) state the general principle: hosts must obtain guest permission before accessing the listing during a reservation, with an exception for emergencies.

Other platforms and local law can set their own conditions, and a stay of several weeks may fall under rental rules that differ from a weekend booking. This is not legal advice; check what applies to the reservation and the location.

Ask in the booking conversation a few days ahead. **Example wording to adapt:** "The cleaning visit we agreed is planned for Tuesday between 10:00 and 12:00. One cleaner will come for about 90 minutes to change the bed linen and towels and clean the kitchen and bathroom. Is that time still fine for you? If you prefer another day, or would rather skip this visit, tell me here." This is sample wording, not a real guest exchange.

Three rules keep the visit clean:

1. Silence is not consent. No reply means the visit is moved, not carried out.
2. A guest may decline. Offer to leave a clean linen set at the door and note the refusal with its date.
3. The cleaner knocks or rings first, even with permission and a working door code.

The visit is not a covert inspection. The cleaner notes what is visible in the course of the work, such as a dripping tap or a broken blind, and does not open cupboards or photograph personal belongings. If something is damaged, the [damage record routine](/blog/guest-damage-found-after-checkout) applies, and the conversation with the guest stays in the booking thread.

## Write the visit down as its own task

In RentTools, cleaning days appear automatically after each checkout. A visit in the middle of a reservation has no checkout behind it, so nothing will create it for you. The same is true of most plans built from a calendar: one long booking produces one cleaning, at the end.

Add each mid-stay visit by hand to the plan your cleaner actually reads, with:

- The date and the agreed time window.
- The scope from the table above.
- The guest's answer and when it arrived.
- Who is going and how they enter.

A RentTools cleaning record holds a date, a status and notes. The note is the place for the scope and the guest's confirmation. It does not message the guest or ask for permission on your behalf.

Long stays change more often than short ones. If the guest extends or leaves early, the visits move with the dates; go through the [date-change checklist](/blog/reservation-date-change-cleaning-checklist) and update each visit rather than only the final cleaning.

## Plan the departure cleaning as a bigger job

Five weeks of cooking and showers leave more behind than three nights. Do not assume the final cleaning fits the usual turnover window. **Illustrative example:** if a normal turnover is planned at 3 hours, ask the cleaner for an estimate after the last mid-stay visit and book the time they name, not the default. These numbers are assumptions, not measurements.

A long-stay guest may also leave on a different schedule than the booking shows. [Confirm the departure](/blog/checkout-confirmation-cleaning-handover) before the cleaner travels, and decide whether a same-day arrival after a long stay is realistic before the following dates are sold.

A mid-stay visit works when the guest agreed to it, the cleaner knows its limits and the date is written where the whole team can see it.
