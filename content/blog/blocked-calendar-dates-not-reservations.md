You notice a closed stretch in your shared rental calendar, but cannot find a guest attached to it. Opening those nights might recover availability—or remove a hold that is still protecting a repair, an owner visit or another stay.

The useful question is what created the block and what must be true before it can end. This guide is a review workflow for hosts using several calendars. The situations below are illustrative, not accounts of real properties.

## Find the reason in the originating calendar

Start with the property, affected nights and calendar source. Open that property's calendar on the originating platform and inspect the dates. Then check its reservation list for a matching confirmed stay, pending request or change. Use the current status, not an old notification.

Airbnb lists availability settings, pending requests and linked calendars among [reasons nights may be blocked](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3612). Select a night in Airbnb to inspect its explanation.

If the dates came from another calendar, follow that connection back to its source. An imported block can be a copy of unavailability created elsewhere. If someone on your team made the hold, ask for its purpose and release condition before assuming it is forgotten.

When the source is unclear, leave a specific review task: which property, which nights, who will investigate and when. A missing guest name is not enough evidence to reopen the property.

## Read a synced block as availability information

Airbnb explains that [booked and manually blocked nights can both sync](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/99), while some external manual blocks may not import. Vrbo likewise describes imported information as [reservations or blocked dates](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-import-my-iCal-or-Google-calendar).

That means a generic calendar label cannot, by itself, tell you whether there is a confirmed guest, whether somebody will occupy the property, or why it is unavailable. Confirm those facts in the source booking or with the person responsible for the hold.

Keep three questions separate in your working notes: **can the night be sold, is there an actual stay, and what work is required?** A maintenance hold can close a night without creating an arrival. An owner visit can require a departure clean without being a guest booking. A confirmed reservation needs the guest handover as well as availability protection.

Keep any explanation you establish with your team's operational notes; do not assume an imported title contains the whole story.

## Match the next action to the actual situation

Consider these hypothetical cases:

- **Repair hold:** the contractor says the visit is complete, but the final property check is still outstanding. Keep the hold under review until the responsible person confirms readiness. A finished appointment alone does not establish that guests can arrive.
- **Owner visit:** the owner leaves tomorrow and another arrival follows. Confirm departure, access and cleaning just as you would for any occupied stay. The absence of a guest reservation does not make the preparation disappear.
- **Pending request:** the calendar is closed while the platform shows an unresolved request. Check that status before treating it as a confirmed arrival or releasing the nights. Assign someone to follow up rather than making a guess from the calendar colour.

For preparation holds around real stays, the [buffer-day guide](/blog/cleaning-buffer-days) explains the scheduling side. If the reason is understood but only the date boundary looks wrong, use the separate [checkout-day blocking guide](/blog/ical-checkout-day-blocked). These are different checks from establishing whether a stay exists.

## Reopen after resolving the reason

Before changing availability, confirm that no actual reservation or continuing restriction still needs those nights. Establish that the owner visit, repair or other hold has ended and that the property can meet the next arrival commitment. Use the platform's supported controls; if a restriction cannot be changed there, investigate its stated cause or contact support.

Do not clear a mystery span by deleting the entire calendar connection. That can remove protection for unrelated dates while leaving the original cause unexplained. Work on the identified reason and check the affected nights afterwards.

Inspect every channel on which the property is sold after the correction. Record what changed and whether each calendar now shows the intended availability. If one destination still differs, keep a follow-up task open and protect nights you cannot honour rather than declaring the review complete.

## Leave a short explanation for the next person

For each non-guest hold, keep its reason, responsible person, release condition and next review date. Add a preparation task when the property needs one. Those notes help a co-host avoid repeating the investigation or reopening a necessary block during a routine calendar check.
