The contractor can finish on Thursday, so you are about to open Thursday night. But the appointment ends in the afternoon, the cleaner is coming on Friday, and nobody has checked the home after the work. A repair end date and a bookable arrival date answer different questions.

For planned maintenance, treat closure as a small operational handover: which nights cannot be sold, who prepares the property afterwards and who confirms it can reopen. This guide concerns availability and preparation, not technical repair instructions or the treatment of an existing guest reservation.

## Define the unavailable nights and the release condition

Before scheduling work, review confirmed stays and pending changes on every booking platform. A new calendar block does not cancel an existing reservation or resolve a conflict with an occupied home. If work affects a booked stay, address that reservation and the guest conversation separately through the relevant platform.

Write down the property, unavailable nights, planned work window, preparation visit and person responsible for reopening. Use a condition such as “inspection and cleaning confirmed” rather than relying only on a contractor's expected finish time. Give the team a time to review progress if the work slips.

**Illustrative example:** the home is empty from Monday morning. Work runs Tuesday to Thursday, cleaning and a readiness check finish Friday at 10:00, and the next proposed arrival is Friday at 16:00. Initially close Monday through Friday nights: five nights. Keep Friday night closed until the Friday check confirms readiness, then release that night if the proposed arrival still fits. This example assumes there is no existing reservation in that period; the times are planning assumptions, not a recommended repair allowance.

## Close availability and verify each channel

Use the supported availability controls on every channel where the home can be booked. Airbnb's [calendar instructions](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/447) let hosts select nights and mark them blocked. Confirm the correct listing and the first and last affected nights before saving.

RentTools supports manually closed dates, and its outgoing calendar includes those dates as blocked events. You can use that to represent the maintenance hold in your working calendar. It is an availability block, not a guest reservation or a work order for a contractor.

Do not assume the block has already reached every platform. Airbnb's [sync guidance](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/99) says externally blocked nights may or may not import, and receiving calendars depend on their own update settings. Open each selling channel and inspect the affected nights. Where closure has not arrived, protect the dates using that channel's supported controls and record that additional hold so it can be reviewed later.

Check the actual dates, not just a successful sync timestamp. If the boundary looks wrong, the [checkout-day guide](/blog/ical-checkout-day-blocked) helps distinguish an overnight interval from a displayed departure day. Do not disconnect an entire calendar to manage one maintenance window.

## Keep the preparation handover separate

A closed calendar does not tell a cleaner what work remains. Agree who will check the property after the contractor leaves, what preparation is required and when access is available. Name the deadline for confirming readiness and the person who receives that confirmation.

Review any existing cleaning instruction for those dates. Do not leave an old checkout clean as the only briefing if the actual task is post-work preparation. Equally, do not remove necessary work merely because there is no guest departing. The [cleaning schedule guide](/blog/cleaning-schedule-automation) covers the wider plan; your maintenance brief should identify this particular visit.

If Thursday's finish moves in the example, revisit Friday's preparation and possible arrival before promising the home is available. Keep the hold under review until a responsible person has confirmed the revised plan.

## Reopen only the hold that has ended

At the review point, establish four things:

- The responsible person has confirmed the work is complete and the home is ready for the next agreed use.
- Required cleaning and preparation are finished, with access instructions still correct.
- No reservation, owner stay or separate restriction continues to need the same nights.
- Someone will check the result on every booking channel after the maintenance hold is removed.

Remove the identified temporary closure in RentTools and any matching manual holds you created elsewhere. Do not force dates open merely to override an unexplained block. If another restriction remains, use the [blocked-dates review guide](/blog/blocked-calendar-dates-not-reservations) to establish its source.

Finally, inspect each channel again. Record who reopened which nights and when, and pass the next arrival details to the person preparing the home. The job is complete when the intended nights have the intended availability and the next guest's preparation is owned by someone.
