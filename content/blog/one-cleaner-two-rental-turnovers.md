Two properties have checkout at 11:00 and check-in at 15:00. Each clean looks manageable on its own. Assign both to the same person, though, and the drive between them can consume the last useful margin.

The question is whether one agreed route fits all the deadlines. A cleaner's name beside both properties is only the starting point. This guide helps you review that route before the day gets busy; the timings below are a hypothetical example, not measured industry averages.

## Put the whole working day on one plan

List the properties needing a turnover on the same day. For each, write down when the outgoing guest must leave, when access is actually available, and when the next guest is entitled to check in. Include any separately agreed early arrival or late departure.

Then ask the cleaner about their other jobs and available working hours. You do not need another host's reservation details; you need to know when the person can start, leave and travel. A free slot in your own calendar does not establish their availability.

Use your own recent cleaning durations, including the final inspection. Add tasks that sometimes disappear from the estimate: collecting keys, parking, walking between buildings, taking laundry away and picking up supplies. Allocate each task once, either to the property visit or to the journey.

Airbnb's [ground rules for hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895) require homes to be clean before check-in and cleaned between stays. Plan the route around a property being ready for arrival, with the final check included. For preparing one property's window, see the [guide to cleaning buffer days](/blog/cleaning-buffer-days).

## Test a route with realistic travel and a margin

Try the order with the earliest firm deadline first, then check whether reversing the order helps. A total-hours calculation alone can hide an access restriction at the second property.

**Hypothetical example:** both properties are accessible from 11:00 and must be ready by 15:00. One person handles the whole route. These durations are assumptions, and the cleaning visits already include inspection.

| Route task | Allowance |
| --- | --- |
| A: clean and inspect | 120 minutes |
| Travel and parking | 30 minutes |
| B: clean and inspect | 90 minutes |
| Route contingency | 30 minutes |

Starting at 11:00, A finishes at 13:00. Travel takes the cleaner to B at 13:30, and B finishes at 15:00. The work and travel use all 240 available minutes. Including the assumed contingency requires 270 minutes, taking the plan to 15:30. The proposed route therefore has no room for that margin before check-in.

Do not solve this on paper by silently reducing the cleaning time or treating a guest's estimated late arrival as permission to miss the promised check-in time. If you use a different allowance, explain what changed: a measured shorter task, supplies already on site, or another confirmed worker.

## Resolve the assignment before sending a final schedule

Choose a practical change and check it with the people involved:

- Split the properties between two confirmed cleaners.
- Move a flexible job into a genuinely available earlier or later slot.
- Prepare supplies and spare linen in advance, then re-estimate only the tasks affected.
- For future unbooked dates, leave enough preparation time instead of accepting a turnover you cannot cover.

Airbnb's [hosting-routine guidance](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/how-to-optimize-your-hosting-routine-377) suggests keeping extra bedding and towels for quicker turnarounds. That can remove a laundry dependency; it does not guarantee a particular number of minutes saved. The [linen-inventory guide](/blog/linen-inventory-short-term-rental) helps you plan the supplies side.

For an existing Airbnb reservation, the platform's [host standards](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3057) require guest consent to changes to confirmed booking details. Seek agreement when needed; do not rewrite a check-in commitment to make an overloaded route look feasible.

A configured backup also needs a real availability check. If the same backup is already covering another property, adding their name to the plan has not created an extra pair of hands. Confirm the job, time and access before counting it as covered.

## Share the route and close each property separately

RentTools can flag the same cleaner assigned to multiple properties on one cleaning date and show configured backup hints. This is a date-level check: review time windows, travel and actual staffing with your team. A warning prompts a review; the route may still work when the jobs have enough time between them.

After agreeing the plan, copy or print the current cleaning schedule. Add the agreed order, start windows, ready-by deadlines, access arrangements and the person to contact if the route slips. Ask the cleaner to acknowledge the whole route, not just the first address.

Agree a check-in point early enough to act if the first job runs late. Get a separate completion report for each property after its final check. One finished job does not make the second home ready. If work falls behind, contact the responsible host or manager while there is still time to arrange cover and communicate with the affected guest.
