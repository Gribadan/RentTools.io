Illustrative situation: the departing guest says they have left, but the cleaner finds the key safe empty. The next guest’s instructions still say to collect the key there. A clean home will not fix that mismatch, and a spare key somewhere else is not yet an agreed handover.

Work through the return, the usable access route and the outstanding key-security decision separately. This guide concerns a physical key missing between stays, rather than an arrival-time lock failure or a price comparison of access devices.

## Establish where the key should be

Check the return arrangement agreed for this booking. Was the key meant to be inside the safe, handed to a person or left at another agreed location? Ask the departing guest to confirm where they left it and whether someone in the group may still have the set. Keep the exchange neutral; a missing key is not evidence of theft.

Do not infer that everyone has departed from a checkout date or an empty safe. Use the [departure-confirmation handover](/blog/checkout-confirmation-cleaning-handover) if occupancy is unclear. Airbnb’s [physical-space privacy policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3060) requires guest permission for host entry during a reservation, except in an emergency. Routine cleaning does not establish that exception.

Ask an authorized person to check the agreed return location and the team’s existing handover record. Record what was actually found, who checked and what is still unknown. Do not tell the next guest that the key is back merely because someone has sent a message saying they will return it.

If return is possible before the next arrival, confirm who receives the set and verifies it. A courier estimate or a promise to drop it off is still an open action until the key is physically accounted for.

## Check the spare and the missing key as different problems

Find the reserve set and confirm who can make it available. Have an authorized person verify the intended entry route while keeping a working way back in. Do not test access by leaving the only usable key inside a locked home.

**Illustrative example:** reaching an apartment requires a communal entrance key and an apartment key. A spare that opens only the apartment does not provide the complete route. Check the actual doors and any required fob, rather than counting the keys on the ring.

A usable spare may solve today’s handover, but it does not account for the missing physical key. Changing the key-safe code is a separate action; it does not resolve that missing-key question. Ask the responsible owner or manager to assess what needs doing, and coordinate any shared-building access issue with the responsible building contact.

For example, Schlage’s [manufacturer guidance on rekeying](https://schlage-res.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/40387275191444-How-Do-I-Re-Key-My-Locks-at-home) recommends professional help. Check your own lock’s instructions and obtain authorization for any work. Do not assume one brand’s procedure applies to another, or turn a turnover deadline into an improvised lock repair.

If usable access or readiness remains unresolved, follow the [maintenance-hold and reopening check](/blog/maintenance-calendar-hold-reopening) for the separate availability decision. Tell the incoming guest promptly about any change to the agreed access arrangement through the booking conversation. Do not promise a new method until the person, place and timing are confirmed.

## Close the access handover, not just the cleaning visit

RentTools cleaning records have a date, status and notes. Use the relevant record to keep the missing-key issue, responsible person and next check beside the visit. Keep door codes and detailed access credentials out of a general coordination note. The record does not track key custody, change locks or confirm that a teammate read the plan.

If cleaning itself is complete, say so without presenting that status as proof of guest-ready access. If another person must receive a key or meet the next guest, get their acknowledgement directly. After any agreed change, check the instructions the incoming guest will use and send the confirmed arrangement in the booking conversation.

Before closing the issue, confirm three concrete outcomes:

1. The guest’s usable key set is at the agreed handover point or with the confirmed recipient.
2. Someone has verified the full entry route and the responsible person has resolved the outstanding missing-key action.
3. The incoming guest and the team have the same current access arrangement.

If any outcome is still unknown, retain an owner and next check rather than a blanket “ready”. For the next turnover, [open RentTools](/onboard) and add a concise access follow-up beside the cleaning visit. Close it when the handover is verified, not when a spare has merely been located.
