A co-host relationship rarely ends on the day a contract says it does. The person stops answering messages on Friday, still holds full access to the listing on Monday, and the lockbox code they set in spring opens the door in autumn. Removal is not one button. It is a short list of access points, closed in an order that does not leave a confirmed guest without a reply.

This guide covers that handover: what to inventory, how removal works on Airbnb, where Booking.com access actually lives, and how to protect stays that are already booked. It does not cover what you owe a co-host under your own agreement; settle that separately before you start.

## Inventory access before you remove it

Write down every place the person can read, change or open something. Removing access in a hurry tends to miss the places that are not on a platform.

- **Platform roles:** Airbnb co-host permissions, Booking.com extranet user accounts, and any Vrbo access you granted.
- **Shared logins:** any account where they used your own email and password instead of their own user.
- **Your tools:** channel manager, calendar tool, messaging templates, cleaning schedule, shared spreadsheets.
- **Calendar feeds:** iCal export links they copied into their own tools.
- **The building:** smart lock codes, lockbox codes, physical keys, garage remotes, Wi-Fi.
- **People they brought in:** cleaners they booked, other co-hosts they invited, suppliers who only know their number.

The last line is the one hosts skip. A co-host often becomes the only contact a cleaner or maintenance supplier has for your property.

## Airbnb: remove the co-host and check who they added

Airbnb offers three [co-host permission levels](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1534): full access, calendar and messaging access, and calendar access. Full-access co-hosts can add, remove and change permissions for other co-hosts, except adding another full-access co-host. Airbnb states that the listing owner is responsible for every co-host, including those added by a full-access co-host. Before removing the person, open the co-host list on each listing and note anyone they invited.

The removal itself, according to [Airbnb's removal guidance](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1632), runs from the desktop: open **Listings**, select the listing, go to **Co-hosts** in the listing editor, select the person and choose to remove them, with an optional note. Hosts and full-access co-hosts can do this. Repeat it per listing; a co-host on three listings needs three removals.

After removal, the person can no longer update the listing, handle booking requests, view the calendar or message guests. They also stop receiving co-host payouts for bookings that start after the removal. Airbnb is explicit that you remain responsible for current and future requests, including ones the co-host accepted.

If the departing person was the **primary host**, Airbnb asks you to select a replacement: the listing owner or another eligible full-access co-host. If no full-access co-host remains, the owner becomes primary host automatically. Decide this before you click rather than leaving the platform to pick for you.

If you only want to reduce what someone can do during a notice period, changing permissions is an alternative to removal. Moving them from full access to calendar access, for example, keeps them informed without letting them edit prices or accept requests.

## Booking.com: access is a user account

Booking.com does not use co-hosts. Access to a property runs through extranet user accounts. Booking.com's [account types and access rights article](https://partnerhelp.booking.com/hc/en-gb/articles/213301525) says admin accounts can manage other accounts' access, and there must always be at least one admin. Permissions, including one to add and remove users, are assigned per account.

That gives you two checks. First, find the person's own user account and remove it or strip its permissions. Second, if they ever held **admin** rights or logged in with the main account's credentials, change that password as well. Removing a separate user does nothing if the person also knows the login of the account doing the removing.

For Vrbo, open your account's access or user settings and remove anyone you added. If access was shared through your own login, change the password. The principle is the same on every platform: a separate user can be removed, while a shared login has to be replaced.

## Your tools, calendar links and the door

Platform access is only half the list. In RentTools, managers are added per property through an invite link. A manager can edit reservations, sync calendars and manage cleaning, but cannot delete the property or add other managers. Only the property owner can remove a manager, and that removes their access to that property. Unused invite links expire after seven days and can be revoked before then, so revoke any you sent and never used.

Calendar export links are different. Anyone holding one can keep reading your occupancy even after their account is gone. If the co-host copied your export links into their own tools, rotate them and re-import the new ones where you need them. The [iCal feed security guide](/blog/ical-feed-url-security) explains why that matters and what breaks when you do it.

Then the physical side:

- Change smart lock and lockbox codes the co-host knew, after the last stay they prepared.
- Collect physical keys, or rekey if you cannot account for every copy.
- Change the Wi-Fi password if they had admin access to the router, then follow the [Wi-Fi update checklist](/blog/wifi-details-after-password-change) so guest instructions do not go stale.

A [smart lock](/blog/smart-lock-vs-lockbox-cost-math) makes this step a settings change rather than a locksmith visit, which is one argument for it if your team changes more than once a year.

## Protect the stays already booked

The order matters more than the speed. Remove access only after the people who depend on the co-host know who replaces them.

**Illustrative example.** A host has two listings. The co-host's last day is the 15th. Between the 15th and the 30th there are four confirmed stays: two arrive while the co-host is still working, two after. A workable sequence:

1. Before the 15th, send each of the four guests one message from the platform thread confirming the arrival details and the contact who will answer from now on.
2. Tell the cleaner and any supplier, in writing, who books and approves work after the 15th.
3. On the 15th, remove the co-host on Airbnb, the extranet user on Booking.com and the manager role in your tools.
4. After the co-host's last prepared turnover, change the lock codes and send the new ones only to the next guest.
5. The next day, open each message thread and calendar to confirm nothing is waiting on the removed person.

Step five catches the gaps. A guest may have replied to the old co-host's last message, or a request may have been pending when access ended. On Airbnb those remain yours to answer.

## Keep a one-page offboarding record

Finish with a short record per departure: the date, every access point from the inventory, who removed it and when, which links and codes changed, and who now owns each upcoming stay. It takes ten minutes to write and it is the first thing you will want if a former co-host later says they never lost access.
